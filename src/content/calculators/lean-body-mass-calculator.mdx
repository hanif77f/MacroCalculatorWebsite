# Quick Answer: Lean Body Mass

Use the Lean Body Mass Calculator to estimate how much of your body weight is lean mass using your age, sex, height, and weight. It compares three published equations—Boer, James, and Hume—so you can see how the estimate changes by method.

Lean body mass is the portion of body weight that is not body fat. It includes muscle, bone, organs, connective tissue, and body water, along with a small amount of essential fat depending on the body-composition model. Treat the result as a practical reference, not a direct measurement of muscle tissue.

---

# What Is Lean Body Mass?

Lean body mass (LBM) estimates the part of your body weight that is not stored body fat. In practical terms, it includes your muscles, bones, organs, connective tissues, and water. It is therefore broader than muscle mass alone.

The term lean body weight is often used in calculator searches as a synonym for lean body mass. The terminology is not perfectly standardized, so it helps to explain the relationship rather than treating every term as a separate measurement.

> **Important distinction:** Lean body mass is not the same as skeletal muscle mass. A person can have a relatively high LBM without all of that mass being muscle.

---

# Why Calculate Lean Body Mass?

LBM can be useful when you want a body-composition estimate that looks beyond total scale weight. It can help you follow changes in lean tissue and body composition during a weight-loss, maintenance, or resistance-training phase.

LBM is also used as an input or reference in some nutrition and metabolic calculations. For example, body-composition-based equations such as Katch-McArdle use lean body mass to estimate basal metabolic rate. That makes an LBM result a useful supporting metric alongside a BMR or TDEE calculator.

For medical dosing or clinical decisions, do not treat a consumer calculator estimate as a substitute for a measured or professionally assessed body-composition value.

# How Is Lean Body Mass Calculated?

Several published equations estimate lean body mass from simple measurements. This calculator compares Boer, James, and Hume equations. Because the equations were developed from different populations and methods, they can produce somewhat different results for the same person.

## Boer Formula

The Boer equations estimate lean body mass from body weight and height and use separate coefficients for men and women. Weight is entered in kilograms and height in centimeters.

**Men: LBM = 0.407 × weight (kg) + 0.267 × height (cm) − 19.2**

**Women: LBM = 0.252 × weight (kg) + 0.473 × height (cm) − 48.3**

## James Formula

The James equations also use height and weight, with the weight-to-height relationship squared. They are commonly included in lean body mass calculators for comparison with other prediction equations.

**Men: LBM = 1.1 × weight − 128 × (weight ÷ height)²**

**Women: LBM = 1.07 × weight − 148 × (weight ÷ height)²**

Weight is in kilograms and height in centimeters.

## Hume Formula

The Hume equations predict lean body mass from height and weight with sex-specific coefficients. The original work was developed from estimates of lean body mass derived from total body-water measurements.

**Men: LBM = 0.32810 × weight (kg) + 0.33929 × height (cm) − 29.5336**

**Women: LBM = 0.29569 × weight (kg) + 0.41813 × height (cm) − 43.2933**

# Lean Body Mass From Body Fat Percentage

If you already have a reliable body-fat percentage, a simple body-composition calculation can estimate lean body mass by subtracting fat mass from total body weight.

**LBM = body weight × (1 − body fat percentage as a decimal)**

Use body fat as a decimal. For example, 20% = 0.20.

This method is mathematically straightforward, but the result depends directly on the accuracy of the body-fat percentage you entered. A consumer scale or visual estimate can therefore introduce substantial uncertainty into the LBM result.

For a separate estimate of body-fat percentage, use the [Body Fat Calculator](/calculators/body-fat-calculator) rather than treating this page as a second body-fat calculator.

# Lean Body Mass vs Lean Body Weight

Lean body mass and lean body weight are common search terms for essentially the same practical question: how much of your body weight is lean rather than fat. Different sources may use the terminology differently, so this page uses “lean body mass” as the primary term while acknowledging the variation.

# Lean Body Mass vs Fat-Free Mass

Lean body mass and fat-free mass are often treated as interchangeable in everyday fitness discussions, but body-composition terminology can differ by model. Fat-free mass excludes all fat, including essential fat, whereas lean body mass can include some essential fat associated with tissues and organs.

For a general consumer calculator, the most important point is consistency: the term used in the result should match the formula and definition being applied. Do not present LBM as a direct measurement of all muscle tissue.

---

# Lean Body Mass vs Muscle Mass

Muscle mass is one component of lean body mass, not the whole of it. Lean body mass also includes bone, body water, organs, connective tissue, and other non-fat components.

This distinction matters because searches for “muscle mass calculator” can look similar to LBM searches. An LBM estimate does not tell you exactly how many kilograms or pounds of skeletal muscle you have.

The published equations use different coefficients for men and women because body-composition relationships differ between the groups used to develop and validate the equations. The calculator asks for sex before applying the equations.

## Lean Body Mass for Men

For men, the Boer, James, and Hume equations each produce an LBM estimate from height and body weight. Because body composition varies substantially among individuals, treat the result as a range or method comparison rather than a precise measurement.

## Lean Body Mass for Women

For women, the same three published equations use different coefficients. The result is an estimate of lean body mass, not a target body weight or a recommended body-fat percentage.

# How Age Affects Lean Body Mass

Lean body mass tends to change with age, but the size and pattern of those changes vary from person to person. Activity level, resistance training, nutrition, illness, and changes in body weight can all influence body composition over time.

Age is included in the calculator interface because age-related body-composition differences matter when interpreting the estimate, even though the adult Boer, James, and Hume formulas primarily use height and weight in their equations.

---

# Lean Body Mass and Weight Loss

During weight loss, total body weight can decrease because of changes in fat mass, lean mass, glycogen, and body water. Looking only at the scale can therefore hide what is happening to body composition.

Tracking an estimated LBM over time can provide additional context, but small changes should not be overinterpreted because the estimate has formula and measurement uncertainty. Resistance training, adequate protein intake, and an appropriately managed calorie deficit are common strategies used to support lean-mass retention, but this calculator does not prescribe a specific diet.

---

# Lean Body Mass and Muscle Gain

When gaining weight, changes in body weight can include both lean and fat mass. An LBM estimate can help you follow the direction of body-composition change without assuming that every pound or kilogram gained is muscle.

People focused on muscle growth should combine scale weight with training performance, measurements, progress photos, and other appropriate indicators rather than using LBM as a direct measure of newly built muscle.

---

# Lean Body Mass and BMR

Lean body mass has a direct connection to some metabolic equations. The Katch-McArdle equation, for example, estimates basal metabolic rate using lean body mass rather than total body weight.

That makes LBM useful as a supporting body-composition metric, but it does not replace a BMR or TDEE calculation. Use the dedicated [BMR Calculator](/calculators/bmr-calculator) or [TDEE Calculator](/calculators/tdee-calculator) when the goal is estimating energy expenditure.

# Real-Life Lean Body Mass Examples

The following examples show how the equations can produce different estimates for the same person. The differences do not mean one formula is automatically wrong; they reflect different prediction equations.

## Worked Example: 75 kg Male at 178 cm

Inputs: age 30, male, weight 75 kg, height 178 cm.

| Formula | Calculation | Estimated LBM |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Boer | 0.407 × 75 + 0.267 × 178 − 19.2 | 58.9 kg |
| James | 1.1 × 75 − 128 × (75 ÷ 178)² | 59.8 kg |
| Hume | 0.32810 × 75 + 0.33929 × 178 − 29.5336 | 55.5 kg |

The three equations give a spread of roughly 55.5 to 59.8 kg. That spread is a reminder that estimated LBM is not a laboratory measurement.

## Worked Example: 65 kg Female at 165 cm

Inputs: age 30, female, weight 65 kg, height 165 cm.

| Formula | Estimated LBM |
| --- | ---: |
| Boer | 46.1 kg |
| James | 46.6 kg |
| Hume | 44.9 kg |

The equations give an estimated range of about 44.9–46.6 kg. Reporting the method alongside the result improves transparency.

# How to Measure Lean Body Mass

A calculator estimates LBM from body measurements; it does not directly measure lean tissue. More direct body-composition methods can include dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA), bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA), and other laboratory or clinical methods.

Different measurement methods have different assumptions, costs, and error sources. Even when a method is more sophisticated than a calculator, consistency in the method and testing conditions can be more useful for following change over time than comparing unrelated methods.

---

# What Is a Good Lean Body Mass?

There is no universal “good” LBM number independent of height, sex, age, and total body weight. A single LBM value should be interpreted within the broader context of body composition rather than judged by a standalone cutoff.

For general fitness planning, trends over time are often more informative than trying to reach a fixed LBM number. The calculator emphasizes estimation and context rather than labeling a result as good or bad.

---

# How Accurate Is a Lean Body Mass Calculator?

Accuracy depends on both the equation and the person being estimated. Anthropometric formulas are prediction equations, so they can systematically overestimate or underestimate lean mass in particular populations.

Validation studies of the Hume equation have found meaningful agreement with DXA in some populations while also showing average differences between estimated and measured lean mass. LBM calculator results are useful estimates, not exact measurements.

> **Best practice:** Use the same formula and measurement units when monitoring yourself over time, and avoid treating small changes as meaningful unless they persist across repeated measurements.

# Common Lean Body Mass Calculation Mistakes

## Using Mixed Units

Entering pounds where a formula expects kilograms or inches where it expects centimeters can make the estimate completely wrong. This calculator converts entered measurements to the units required by each formula.

## Confusing LBM With Muscle Mass

Lean body mass contains much more than skeletal muscle, so the result should not be described as total muscle weight.

## Treating One Equation as Exact

Different published equations can produce different estimates for the same person.

## Changing Methods Between Measurements

Switching from Boer to Hume, for example, can look like a body-composition change even when your body did not change.

## Overinterpreting Small Changes

Hydration, food intake, and normal measurement variation can affect body weight and therefore the estimate.

## Using an Unreliable Body-Fat Estimate

A body-fat-based LBM calculation is only as reliable as the body-fat percentage used.

## Using LBM as a Medical Measurement

Clinical decisions should rely on appropriate assessment by qualified professionals rather than a consumer calculator estimate.

# When Should You Recalculate Lean Body Mass?

Recalculate when your body weight or height changes meaningfully, or when you want to review progress after a sustained change in body composition. For routine tracking, monthly or periodic checks are generally more useful than recalculating every day.

For the clearest trend, use similar measurement conditions each time and keep the same formula. The goal is to compare like with like.
