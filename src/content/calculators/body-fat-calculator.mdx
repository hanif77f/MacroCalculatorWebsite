# Quick Answer: Body Fat Percentage

Use the Body Fat Calculator to estimate your body fat percentage from simple body measurements. It uses the U.S. Navy circumference method, a practical estimate of body composition that does not require specialized equipment.

Body fat percentage is the proportion of your total body weight estimated to be fat mass. It can provide context that body weight or BMI alone cannot, but a tape-based calculation is an estimate rather than a direct laboratory measurement or medical diagnosis.

The calculator also estimates body fat mass and [lean body mass](/calculators/lean-body-mass-calculator) from your weight and body-fat percentage. Women enter hip circumference in addition to height, neck, and waist measurements.

---

# What Is Body Fat Percentage?

Body fat percentage tells you how much of your total body weight is estimated to be fat. For example, an estimate of 20% means that about 20% of total body mass is fat mass, while the remaining mass is fat-free or lean mass.

Body fat is not something the body should eliminate completely. Essential fat supports normal physiological functions, while storage fat provides energy reserves and other functions. The amount and distribution of body fat vary substantially between individuals and are influenced by sex, age, genetics, activity, nutrition, and other factors.

Because body weight also includes muscle, bone, water, organs, and other tissues, body-fat percentage can sometimes provide useful information that scale weight alone cannot.

# How Does the Body Fat Calculator Work?

The calculator uses the U.S. Navy circumference method. Rather than measuring body fat directly, it applies a regression equation to body circumference measurements and height to estimate body-fat percentage.

## U.S. Navy Circumference Method

The U.S. Navy equations were developed from body-composition research at the Naval Health Research Center by Hodgdon and Beckett. Men use height, neck, and waist measurements; women also enter hip circumference.

## What Measurements Are Needed?

| Measurement | Men | Women | Practical instruction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Height | Yes | Yes | Measure standing height consistently. |
| Weight | Yes | Yes | Use the selected unit throughout. |
| Neck | Yes | Yes | Measure below the larynx. |
| Waist | Yes | Yes | Men: around the navel; women: at the narrowest point. |
| Hip | No | Yes | Measure around the widest horizontal point of the hips. |

## Why Do Men and Women Use Different Measurements?

The equations were developed separately because body-fat distribution and the relationships between circumferences and body density differ. The female equation therefore includes hip circumference, while the male equation does not.

# How to Measure Body Fat With a Tape Measure

The tape-measure method only works as well as the measurements entered. Small differences in tape placement or tension can change the estimate, so consistency matters more than trying to make a single measurement look perfect.

## Measure Your Height

Stand upright without shoes on a firm, level surface. Keep your head in a neutral position and record your height in the selected unit.

## Measure Your Neck

Place the tape around the neck just below the larynx. Keep the tape level and snug without compressing the skin. Use the same position each time you repeat the measurement.

## Measure Your Waist

For the U.S. Navy method, men measure around the abdomen at the level of the navel. Women measure at the level of the smallest natural waist circumference. Do not pull the stomach inward.

## Measure Your Hips for Women

Measure the hips at the largest horizontal circumference. Keep the tape level around the body rather than allowing it to angle upward or downward.

## Keep Measurement Conditions Consistent

For progress tracking, repeat measurements under similar conditions. The same time of day, similar hydration, similar clothing, and the same tape position can reduce avoidable variation.

# How to Calculate Body Fat Percentage

The U.S. Navy equations use base-10 logarithms. This calculator converts measurements to centimeters and applies the documented metric equations, so the same method is used for both unit selections.

## Formula for Men

**BF% = 495 ÷ [1.0324 − 0.19077 × log10(waist − neck) + 0.15456 × log10(height)] − 450**

The metric equation uses centimeters for length inputs. Waist is measured at the navel.

## Formula for Women

**BF% = 495 ÷ [1.29579 − 0.35004 × log10(waist + hip − neck) + 0.22100 × log10(height)] − 450**

The metric equation uses centimeters for length inputs and includes hip circumference.

# Body Fat Mass and Lean Body Mass

Once body-fat percentage has been estimated, the calculator can translate it into approximate fat mass and lean mass. These values are derived from body weight and estimated body-fat percentage; they are not independently measured.

## Body Fat Mass Formula

**Fat mass = body weight × (body fat % ÷ 100)**

## Lean Body Mass Formula

**Lean mass = body weight − fat mass**

Lean mass is the portion of body weight left after subtracting estimated fat mass. It includes multiple tissues and fluids, so it should not be treated as a direct measurement of skeletal muscle.

# Body Fat Percentage for Men

Men generally have a lower essential body-fat requirement than women, which is reflected in many reference tables. The ACE categories place male essential fat at 2–5%, athlete levels at 6–13%, fitness at 14–17%, and average nonathlete levels at 18–24%.

## Essential Fat

Essential fat supports normal physiological function. Very low body-fat levels are not automatically healthier, and attempting to stay near the essential-fat range can carry risks.

## Athletes

Athletes and highly trained individuals may fall into lower body-fat ranges than the general population, but no single percentage defines athletic performance. Sport, training volume, genetics, and individual physiology all matter.

## Fitness and Average Ranges

The ACE reference table lists 14–17% as a fitness category and 18–24% as average for men. These labels are useful context, not medical classifications.

---

# Body Fat Percentage for Women

Women typically have higher essential and reference body-fat percentages than men. The ACE table lists 10–13% as essential fat, 14–20% for athletes, 21–24% for fitness, and 25–31% as average.

## Essential Fat

Women require a higher proportion of essential fat than men for normal physiological function. Very low body-fat levels can be associated with problems and should not be pursued solely because a calculator produces a lower number.

## Athletes

Some female athletes have lower body-fat percentages than the general population, but athlete status should not be reduced to a single number. Sport demands, menstrual function, energy availability, recovery, and overall health matter.

## Fitness and Average Ranges

The ACE reference table lists 21–24% as fitness and 25–31% as average for women. These are reference categories, not universal health targets.

# What Is a Healthy Body Fat Percentage?

There is no single body-fat percentage that is automatically healthy for every person. Reference ranges differ by sex, age, population, measurement method, and the purpose of the assessment. A number appropriate for one person may not be an appropriate target for another.

## Why There Is No Single Ideal Number

Body-fat percentage is only one part of body composition. Fat distribution, waist circumference, cardiorespiratory fitness, strength, diet, sleep, medical history, and other health measures provide important context. Body-fat percentage also varies with measurement error.

## How Sex and Age Affect the Reference Range

Sex is built directly into the U.S. Navy equations because men and women use different circumference relationships. Age does not appear in the Navy circumference equation itself, but it matters when interpreting reference values and in some alternative BMI-based formulas.

# Body Fat Examples

Worked examples show how tape measurements flow through the equations. These are demonstrations, not recommended targets.

## Worked Example: Man

Suppose a man is 70 inches tall, has a 15-inch neck, and a 34-inch waist. Waist minus neck is 19 inches; applying the U.S. Navy equation gives an estimate of about 17.4% with this calculator's metric implementation.

If he weighs 180 lb, estimated fat mass is about 31.4 lb and estimated lean mass is about 148.6 lb.

**180 × 0.1743 ≈ 31.4 lb fat; 180 − 31.4 ≈ 148.6 lb lean mass**

## Worked Example: Woman

Suppose a woman is 64 inches tall, has a 15-inch neck, a 29-inch waist, and 40-inch hips. Waist plus hip minus neck is 54 inches; applying the female Navy equation gives an estimated body fat of about 27.7% with this calculator's metric implementation.

If she weighs 140 lb, estimated fat mass is about 38.7 lb and estimated lean mass about 101.3 lb.

# U.S. Navy vs BMI Body Fat Estimates

A second estimate can be calculated from BMI, age, and sex. This optional comparison uses different inputs from the circumference method, so different equations can produce different estimates for the same person.

## BMI-Based Estimate

For adults, one commonly published BMI-based body-fat equation is:

**Adult men: BFP = 1.20 × BMI + 0.23 × age − 16.2**

**Adult women: BFP = 1.20 × BMI + 0.23 × age − 5.4**

A BMI-based body-fat estimate is still an estimate. BMI does not directly measure body fat or distinguish fat mass from lean tissue.

## Why the Two Estimates May Differ

The Navy method uses height and circumferences, while the BMI method uses body weight, height, age, and sex. Since they are built from different inputs and regression assumptions, agreement is not guaranteed. Differences do not necessarily mean one result is wrong.

# How to Measure Body Fat at Home

A tape measure is one of the most accessible ways to estimate body fat at home, but it is not the only method. Each body-composition technique has different trade-offs in cost, convenience, and measurement error.

## Tape Measure

A flexible tape is inexpensive and easy to repeat. It works well for tracking personal trends when you consistently follow the same measurement protocol.

## Skinfold Calipers

Skinfold methods estimate body fat from the thickness of subcutaneous fat at specific sites. Accuracy depends on the protocol, equation, and skill of the person taking the measurements.

## Bioelectrical Impedance

Bioelectrical impedance devices estimate body composition from electrical measurements. Results can vary with hydration, food intake, skin temperature, and device characteristics.

## DEXA and Hydrostatic Methods

DEXA and hydrostatic weighing are specialized approaches used for body-composition assessment. They can provide more detail than a simple tape-based calculator but are less accessible and still involve assumptions and measurement considerations.

# How Accurate Is a Body Fat Calculator?

A body-fat calculator can be mathematically precise while biologically imperfect. The formula can produce a repeatable result from the numbers you enter, but the result remains an estimate of a complex trait.

## Sources of Error in Tape Measurements

Tape placement, tape tension, posture, breathing, clothing, time of day, hydration, and abdominal fullness can all affect circumference measurements. A small input change can shift the estimate.

## Why Results Differ From DEXA or Other Tests

Different methods measure or estimate body composition in different ways. A circumference equation is a regression estimate; it does not directly measure the amount of fat in your tissues.

## Best Use: Tracking Trends

For many people, the best use of a home calculator is trend tracking. Repeating the same method under similar conditions can show whether the estimate is moving up or down over time, even when the absolute number has some uncertainty.

# Common Body Fat Calculation Mistakes

## Measuring the Wrong Sites

Using the wrong waist position or placing the neck or hip tape differently from the documented method can produce a different estimate even when the person has not changed.

## Holding Your Breath or Pulling Your Stomach In

Do not intentionally pull the abdomen inward to make the waist smaller. Follow the same relaxed measurement procedure each time.

## Using Different Units

The formula constants depend on the unit system. Make sure the calculator knows whether the supplied measurement is in inches or centimeters and converts it consistently.

## Changing Measurement Conditions

Comparing a morning measurement before breakfast with an evening measurement after meals can create apparent changes that are partly due to normal short-term variation.

## Comparing Different Methods

A Navy estimate, smart-scale estimate, skinfold estimate, and DEXA result should not be expected to match exactly. Pick one method for routine tracking and use other methods as separate assessments.

## Treating the Estimate as Exact

Avoid interpreting a result such as 17.5% as if it were laboratory-measured to the nearest tenth. Decimal places are a display choice, not a guarantee of measurement precision.

# Body Fat Percentage and Weight Loss

During weight loss, scale weight can fall because of changes in fat, muscle, glycogen, water, and digestive contents. A body-fat estimate can add context, especially when you track it consistently rather than reacting to a single reading.

## Tracking Changes in Body Composition

A useful progress view can combine body weight, waist circumference, training performance, and estimated body-fat trends. None of these measures is perfect on its own.

## Body Weight Is Not the Same as Body Fat

Two people can weigh the same amount and have different body-fat percentages because their fat mass and lean mass differ. This is one reason body composition can be useful alongside body weight.

---

# Body Fat Percentage and Muscle Gain

During muscle gain, scale weight can increase from muscle tissue, fat tissue, water, and glycogen. Watching only the scale can make it difficult to tell what is changing.

## Why Scale Weight Alone Can Mislead

A gradual increase in body weight can be useful for gaining muscle, but the composition of that gain matters. A repeated body-fat estimate can provide another data point when evaluating progress.

## Using Lean Mass Alongside Body Fat Percentage

Because fat mass and lean mass are derived from body weight and estimated body-fat percentage, they can help translate a percentage change into approximate kilograms or pounds. Derived lean mass is not the same as measured skeletal muscle mass.

# When Should You Recalculate Body Fat?

There is no need to recalculate body fat every day. Daily readings can create noise because tape measurements and body water fluctuate. For progress tracking, a consistent schedule such as every few weeks can be more useful than constant checking.

Use the same method, the same measurement sites, and similar conditions before comparing results.

# Body Fat Calculator for Men and Women

The calculator can be used by both men and women, but the U.S. Navy equations are sex-specific. Men use height, neck, and waist measurements; women use height, neck, waist, and hip measurements.

Do not use the male equation with female measurements or vice versa. The equations are different by design, so the interface makes the required fields clear based on the selected sex.

# Safety and Limitations

Body-fat estimates can be useful, but a calculator should not diagnose a health condition or determine whether a person is medically healthy from one percentage alone. Very low body-fat targets can also be inappropriate or unsafe for some people.

For medical evaluation, significant unexplained changes in body composition, or decisions that depend on accurate body-composition measurement, consult an appropriately qualified health professional. More specialized methods may be appropriate depending on the situation.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## What is a body fat calculator?

A body fat calculator estimates your body-fat percentage from measurements such as height, neck, waist, hip, weight, age, and sex, depending on the method. The U.S. Navy method uses circumference measurements and is an estimate rather than a direct measurement.

## How do I calculate my body fat percentage?

The U.S. Navy method uses height, neck, waist, and, for women, hip circumference. The calculator applies a sex-specific equation to those measurements and returns an estimated percentage.

## How do I measure body fat at home?

A tape measure can be used with the U.S. Navy circumference method. Other home approaches include skinfold calipers and consumer bioelectrical-impedance devices, but results can vary with technique and conditions.

## What is a good body fat percentage?

There is no single ideal number for everyone. Fitness reference tables use different bands for men and women, and individual health also depends on factors such as fat distribution, fitness, age, and medical context.

## What is a healthy body fat percentage for men?

The ACE table used on this page lists 18–24% as its average category for men. This is a reference band rather than a medical diagnosis.

## What is a healthy body fat percentage for women?

The ACE reference table lists 25–31% as average and 21–24% as fitness for women. These are reference categories, not universal health targets.

## What is the difference between body fat percentage and BMI?

BMI relates weight to height and does not directly measure body fat. Body-fat percentage estimates the proportion of body weight that is fat. Both are indirect tools with limitations.

## Is the Navy body fat calculator accurate?

It can provide a useful estimate, especially when measurements are taken consistently, but it is not as precise as specialized body-composition assessment. Measurement error and individual differences can affect the result.

Women need a different equation within the same calculator because the U.S. Navy method uses hip circumference in the female equation and different constants.

## Do men have lower body fat percentages than women?

On average, men have lower body-fat percentages and lower essential-fat requirements than women. This is reflected in sex-specific reference ranges and equations.

## Does age affect body fat percentage?

Age can influence body composition and how reference values are interpreted. The U.S. Navy circumference equation itself does not include age, while some alternative BMI-based equations do.

## How often should I measure body fat?

Every few weeks is generally more useful for trend tracking than daily measurement. Use the same method and similar conditions so changes are easier to interpret.

## Why does my body fat percentage change when my weight barely changes?

Body-fat estimates also depend on circumference measurements, and those can change because of measurement technique, hydration, food intake, and normal variation. Small changes do not necessarily represent a real change in fat mass.

## Can a smart scale accurately measure body fat?

Smart scales commonly use bioelectrical impedance to estimate body composition. Results can vary with hydration and other conditions, so treat them as estimates and interpret them consistently over time.

## Is DEXA more accurate than a body fat calculator?

DEXA is a specialized body-composition assessment that can provide more detailed information than a simple tape-based equation. It is not necessary or practical for everyone and has its own measurement considerations.

## Can an AI photo estimate body fat percentage?

Photo-based estimates depend on image quality, lighting, pose, and the model. They should not automatically be treated as more accurate than a standardized tape-based method or professional assessment.

## Can I use a body fat calculator for military requirements?

It can explain the general circumference approach and the U.S. Navy equation, but official eligibility or compliance should be checked against current service-specific instructions and measurement rules.

## What should I do if my body fat estimate seems wrong?

Recheck the measurement sites, unit settings, and tape placement. If the result still seems inconsistent, compare it with another standardized method rather than assuming the calculator is exact.

## What is the best way to track body fat over time?

Choose one repeatable method, measure under similar conditions, record the date and measurements, and look at the trend across several readings rather than reacting to a single percentage.
